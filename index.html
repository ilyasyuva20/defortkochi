Must Visit Places

Where history, color, and culture meet

Chinese Fishing Nets

The massive, cantilevered wooden structures with fine nets dipped into the calm water are illuminated by the warm, golden light of early evening. Local fishermen are managing the ropes and nets.

St Francis Church

The whitewashed, colonial-era church, with its distinctive Dutch-influenced facade and two bell towers, stands prominently. Its architecture, a blend of European styles, reflects its long history as the oldest European-built church in India (originally constructed in 1503).

Santa Cruz Basilica

The Santa Cruz Cathedral Basilica in Fort Kochi is one of the oldest and most heritage-rich churches in Kerala. Known for its striking Indo-Gothic architecture, it is famous for its intricate historical frescoes, murals, and a beautiful altar piece that showcases Portuguese and Dutch influences.

Mattancherry Palace

Built by the Portuguese in 1555 and later renovated by the Dutch, the Mattancherry Palace, also known as the Dutch Palace, is a fine example of Kerala architecture mixed with colonial influences. It features a stunning collection of murals depicting scenes from Hindu epics, royal portraits, and artifacts. The palace, with its white walls and red-tiled roofs, is a central landmark in Mattancherry.

Jew Town

A bustling narrow street in Mattancherry, known for its antique shops, spice markets, and historical buildings, including the Paradesi Synagogue (visible in the distance with its distinct blue and white facade and clock tower). The area is a vibrant blend of colonial architecture and lively commerce, popular with tourists. The Paradesi Synagogue, the oldest active synagogue in the Commonwealth, was built in 1568.

Paradesi Synagogue

The Paradesi Synagogue, located in the Jew Town area of Mattancherry, Kochi, is the oldest active synagogue in the Commonwealth of Nations. It is renowned for its architectural beauty, including a floor paved with hand-painted Chinese porcelain tiles, exquisite Belgian chandeliers, and a distinct clock tower. The synagogue serves as a key testament to the Jewish community's long-standing historical presence and heritage in Kerala.

Dutch Cemetery

The Dutch Cemetery in Fort Kochi, established in 1724, is one of the oldest European cemeteries in India. It serves as a historical record of the Dutch and other Europeans who lived and died in Kochi during the 17th and 18th centuries, including many high-ranking officials and traders.

Spice Market

The Mattancherry Spice Market, located in the historic Jew Town area of Kochi, Kerala, is a vibrant and aromatic hub of commerce and heritage. For centuries, it has been a central point for the global spice trade, drawing merchants from around the world to purchase exotic spices like black pepper, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, and ginger directly from local traders.

Dhobhi Khana (traditional laundry)

A Dhobi Khana is a traditional communal laundry, specifically a collective of self-employed laundry workers, or dhobis. The most prominent example is the historic Dhobi Khana located at Veli in Fort Kochi, Kerala.

Fort Kochi Beach

Fort Kochi Beach is a tranquil, historic shoreline situated along the Arabian Sea on the western coast of Kerala. Known for its unique blend of colonial history, lush coastal greenery, and scenic sunset views, the beach offers a relaxed atmosphere that sets it apart from typical commercial beach resorts.

Local chai and street-life experience

Experience the vibrant street life of Fort Kochi with a refreshing cup of local chai, where you can witness the daily rhythms and cultural nuances of this historic district.

Vasco da Gama Square

Vasco da Gama Square is the heart of Fort Kochi, where you can experience the vibrant street life and cultural nuances of this historic district.
