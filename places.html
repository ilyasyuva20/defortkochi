Must Visit Places

Explore Historic Fort Kochi Attractions

Chinese Fishing Nets

The massive, cantilevered wooden structures with fine nets dipped into the calm water are illuminated by the warm, golden light of early evening. Local fishermen are managing the ropes and nets.

St Francis Church

The whitewashed, colonial-era church, with its distinctive Dutch-influenced facade and two bell towers, stands prominently. Its architecture reflects its long history as the oldest European-built church in India (1503).

Santa Cruz Basilica

The Santa Cruz Cathedral Basilica in Fort Kochi is one of the oldest and most heritage-rich churches in Kerala. Known for its striking Indo-Gothic architecture, it is famous for its historical frescoes and murals.

Mattancherry Palace

Built by the Portuguese in 1555 and later renovated by the Dutch, the Mattancherry Palace (Dutch Palace) features a stunning collection of murals depicting scenes from Hindu epics, royal portraits, and artifacts.

Jew Town

A bustling narrow street in Mattancherry, known for its antique shops, spice markets, and historical buildings, including the Paradesi Synagogue with its distinct clock tower.

Paradesi Synagogue

The Paradesi Synagogue in Jew Town is the oldest active synagogue in the Commonwealth of Nations. Renowned for hand-painted Chinese porcelain tiles and Belgian chandeliers.

Dutch Cemetery

The Dutch Cemetery in Fort Kochi, established in 1724, is one of the oldest European cemeteries in India, serving as a historical record of Dutch and European traders.

Spice Market

The Mattancherry Spice Market is a vibrant and aromatic hub of global commerce and heritage, trading black pepper, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, and ginger for centuries.

Dhobhi Khana (traditional laundry)

A Dhobi Khana is a traditional communal laundry collective of self-employed laundry workers. The most prominent example is located at Veli in Fort Kochi, Kerala.

Fort Kochi Beach

Fort Kochi Beach is a tranquil, historic shoreline along the Arabian Sea, known for its unique blend of colonial history, coastal greenery, and scenic sunset views.

Local Chai & Street-Life Experience

Experience the vibrant street life of Fort Kochi with a refreshing cup of local chai, witnessing the daily rhythms and cultural nuances of this historic district.

Vasco da Gama Square

Vasco da Gama Square is the heart of Fort Kochi, where you can experience lively street food stalls, local art, and gentle ocean breezes.

Kerala Heritage

History & Culture Timeline

1
Spice Route Era

Kerala Spice Trade

For thousands of years, Kerala's coast traded black gold pepper and cardamom with Phoenicians, Egyptians, Romans, and Arabs, shaping global history.

2
Colonial Arrivals (1503)

Portuguese & Fort Kochi History

Vasco da Gama arrived, marking the start of European influence. The Portuguese built Fort Emmanuel and St. Francis Church, the oldest European church in India.

3
Royal Splendor (1555)

Mattancherry Palace & Jew Town

Built by the Portuguese as a gift to the King of Kochi, then upgraded by the Dutch (Dutch Palace). Jew Town thrived as a sanctuary for Jewish spice merchants.

4
Colonial Transitions (1663 - 1795)

Dutch & British Influence

The Dutch seized Kochi, turning it into a trading hub, followed by the British in 1795. This rich blend of styles gave Kochi its colonial architectural charm.

5
Living Art Forms

Traditional Arts: Kathakali & Theyyam

Marvel at Kathakali's heavy makeup and dramatic hand gestures, or witness the ritualistic trance dance of Theyyam in North Kerala temple groves.

6
Traditional Backwater Life

Backwater & Houseboat Heritage

Discover the ancient Kettuvallams—traditional wooden boats originally built to transport rice and spices, now converted into luxury floating houses.
